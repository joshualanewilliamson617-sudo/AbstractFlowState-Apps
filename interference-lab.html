The five modules

Double slit
Two coherent sources. Closed-form solution, so it runs at true optical scale. Slit separation, slit width, screen distance, wavelength, and a white-light option.
Diffraction grating
Two to four hundred slits. Adding slits never moves the orders, it only sharpens them — which is what turns a grating into a spectrometer.
Ripple tank
One to six point sources you can drag, with a phase step between them. A phase step steers the beam without anything physically rotating.
Thin film
Soap and oil. A wedge of film rendered in true colour, plus the reflectance maths behind it. Zero thickness comes out black, which is the real dark spot at the top of a draining bubble.
Open field
A different engine. Instead of solving a formula it steps the wave equation on a grid, so anything you build gets solved — including geometry no formula covers.

Open field tools

Select
Drag anything. Barriers slide sideways, individual slits slide up and down, everything stays where you put it.
Point
A point source. Each one carries its own wavelength, phase, amplitude and drift.
Plane
Drag out a line to emit a flat wavefront. This is what you aim into a lens or a mirror.
Probe
A marker that stays put and records a trace. Dropped near a merge ring it snaps onto it exactly.
Erase
Removes whatever you click.
Wall
Drag to draw a solid barrier at any angle. Waves reflect off it and bend around its ends.
Brush
Paint solid obstacles freehand at any shape you like.
Disc
A solid circular obstacle. Watch the wave close back up behind it.
Mirror
A true parabola with its focus marked. A plane wave along the axis collapses onto that dot.
Lens
A region where the wave travels slower. Refraction, focusing and total internal reflection all follow from that one change.

Honest limits

Grid scale
The sandbox holds about twenty to forty wavelengths. A real optical bench spans about two million, which is why the closed-form modules exist alongside it.
Grid anisotropy
Waves travel about 1% faster along the axes than diagonally. Keep ten or more cells per wavelength and it stays negligible — the panel warns you when you drop below that.
Two dimensions
Amplitude falls off as one over the square root of distance, not one over distance. Fringe geometry is right; brightness falloff is not.
Scalar field
One number per cell, so there is no polarization anywhere in the sandbox.
3D preview
Deliberately coarse so it never steals the frame budget from the physics. The export carries the full-resolution field for an offline render.

Live math

Symbols, your numbers, result. Updates on every change.
