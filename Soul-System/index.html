God's-eye views

Rotate: left-drag or one finger. Zoom: scroll or pinch. Slide (pan): right-drag, Shift+drag, or two fingers. Fly: WASD or arrows across the orbit plane, E up, Q down, hold Shift to go faster. The + marks what the camera turns around. Tap a planet to go to it, tap one of its moons to zoom in, tap the moon again to go back to the planet, and tap the planet again to zoom back out to the solar system. Double-tap a planet or moon to zoom in closer. While you're following one, pinch or scroll to zoom and drag to circle it: the camera stays locked on until you press Release camera. Any slide or fly releases the follow.

Bodies
How accurate is this

Planet and Pluto positions come from NASA JPL's Keplerian orbital elements (valid 1800–2050), so they match the real sky for the date shown. Earth's Moon uses a simplified lunar theory. Other moons have their real distances, periods, sizes and orbit planes (tilted to each planet's real pole), but where each sits along its orbit is approximate. The asteroid belt is a statistical scatter. Earth is turned to its true angle for the time shown, and the background is a real all-sky map of the Milky Way, lined up with its true direction.
