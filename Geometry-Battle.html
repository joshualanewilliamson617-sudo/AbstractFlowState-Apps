About the star, the skill slider and the move row

The row of sigils under each form picks which special it fights with. Each sigil is that form's own geometry, and a form starts with its own move — but you can hand any move to any form, so a Tree of Life can open a Torus vortex.

Star a form to back it as your champion. Tap a name to read how it fights.

Skill runs from 1 to 10 and doesn't add mass. A skilled form hunts lighter forms and backs away from heavier ones, recharges its ability faster and hits harder with it, holds its geometry together better, and can take a core up to 15% heavier than its own. At 1 a form just drifts where gravity takes it.

    Moving the camera yourself

    Press the scroll wheel and drag to orbit: sideways turns around the z axis, up and down tilts. Right-drag or Shift-drag does the same. The wheel zooms toward the pointer, left-drag pans. On touch, drag with two fingers to orbit and pinch to zoom.

    When a form takes the field, the capture frames itself — but the moment you scroll or drag, the camera is yours and stays where you put it, so you can go right in on the winner. The result card steps aside as soon as you reach for the field. Press R to hand the frame back.

    The camera is kept on a leash: it won't pan past the edge of the field, zoom out into empty space, or tip past the horizon. Lost anyway? Frame all brings everything back.

    The four acts

    A match is staged rather than simply simulated. The Approach holds gravity back while the forms close, and the camera makes a slow turn of the whole field. The Clash releases it: abilities come round at full rate and the camera cuts to every connection. Duels begins once the field has thinned — the ring tightens, abilities recharge half again as fast, and the camera stops surveying and holds the two heaviest in one frame. Last Stand is the finish: a lead alone will not end it, so the two left have to settle it on the field or on the clock.

    Each act ends on something read off the fight — first contact, how many are standing, how much of the field one form holds — so no two matches are cut the same way. The ring never pushes forms together; it only bleeds off the speed they are drifting apart with, so a thinning field tightens instead of stalemating.

    How a form dies

    Every form is built from bodies held on its true geometry by springs. When bodies from two forms meet slowly, the heavier absorbs the lighter. When they meet fast, both shatter and the debris goes to whichever core has the strongest pull nearby. Gas softens impacts, so gas-rich forms merge where others shatter.

    A form is out when a heavier core swallows its core, or when less than 6% of its geometry is still standing and the core comes unbound. A form that holds 70% of all mass ignites and wins. If time runs out, the heaviest form ignites.

    Any single body over 140 solar masses tears itself apart. That's pair instability, and it's real.

    When a form wins it captures the field: every loose body is drawn into it, the platform takes its colour, and its own geometry unfolds across the whole arena. Each of the nine does that differently, and a form fighting with a borrowed move captures the field with that move's geometry instead.

    Line glow

    Every form is drawn from its own construction: the circles that generate it, the chords between them, the hull it sits in. The lit scene is then blurred once over itself, which is why the lines read as lit tubes rather than flat strokes. The Line glow slider in View sets how far that spreads.

    On a slow device the picture steps itself down rather than dropping frames: the bloom goes first, then line detail on the forms that are small on screen. It comes back on its own when there is room for it.
